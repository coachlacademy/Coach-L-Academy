Step 2
Your Money Story — Where It All Started
Your financial behaviours today were largely shaped before you turned 10. Understanding your money story is the first step to rewriting it.
🧠 What Is Financial Trauma?

Financial trauma is the emotional and psychological damage caused by significant money-related hardship — growing up poor, watching parents fight about bills, having utilities cut off, experiencing sudden financial loss, or living with chronic scarcity. It leaves lasting imprints on how we think, feel, and behave around money as adults.

Scarcity mindset. When money felt unsafe as a child, the brain learns to treat financial resources as permanently unreliable — even when they're not.
Avoidance behaviour. Not opening bank statements. Not checking your balance. Spending to soothe anxiety. These are trauma responses — not character flaws.
Guilt around having. Many people from poor backgrounds feel guilt or shame when they start earning well — as if having money is a betrayal of where they came from.
Self-sabotage. Spending every rand before it can be saved. Making poor decisions when money arrives. Unconsciously recreating scarcity because it feels familiar.

Step 6
Generational Wealth & Black Tax
Building wealth in South Africa as a Black person means navigating both systems and family obligations. You can honour both — with a plan.
💛 What is Generational Wealth?

Generational wealth is assets passed from one generation to the next — property, investments, education, business ownership, and financial knowledge. For most Black South African families, apartheid's deliberate destruction of Black wealth means we are starting this process from zero — or from debt.

It starts with you. You don't need to be a millionaire. The first generation that breaks the cycle of zero — saves, owns property, invests even R200/month — changes the trajectory of every generation after them.
Financial literacy is wealth. Teaching your children about money — how to save, how credit works, how to invest — is the most valuable inheritance you can leave.
Property matters. Homeownership is still one of the most accessible paths to building a balance sheet. Even a modest property transferred to your children is a head start they didn't have.
You cannot build generational wealth while destroying your own financial health. Black tax must be managed — not avoided, but limited, communicated, and planned for.
