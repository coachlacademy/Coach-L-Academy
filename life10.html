Step 2
Burnout Diagnosis — How Burned Out Are You?
Burnout is a clinical condition recognised by the WHO. It has three dimensions. Rate yourself honestly across each one.
🧠 What Is Burnout? (WHO Definition)

Burnout is defined by the World Health Organisation as a syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It is characterised by three dimensions — and all three must be present for a full burnout diagnosis.

1. Exhaustion. Feelings of energy depletion. Not tired-after-a-long-week. Bone-deep depletion that sleep does not fix.
2. Cynicism / Depersonalisation. Increased mental distance from your job. Negative, detached, or indifferent feelings about your work, your colleagues, or your clients.
3. Reduced efficacy. Feelings of reduced professional accomplishment. The sense that nothing you do matters or makes a difference. Difficulty concentrating. Reduced performance despite effort.

🔍 Burnout vs Stress — Know the Difference
Stress is characterised by overengagement — too much emotion, urgency, and reaction. Burnout is characterised by disengagement — emptiness, numbness, and detachment. Stress feels overwhelming. Burnout feels hollow.
Stress produces urgency and anxiety. Burnout produces hopelessness and helplessness. With stress, you still care — too much. With burnout, you have stopped caring, and you feel guilty about not caring.
Stress can be managed with rest, boundaries, and recovery. Burnout requires a more significant intervention — sometimes including time away, professional support, and a fundamental reassessment of your work arrangement.
Step 3
Is Your Workplace Toxic? — Know the Signs
Not all burnout is your fault. Some environments are genuinely harmful. Naming what is happening is the first step to deciding what to do about it.
⚖️ Your Labour Rights in South Africa
The Labour Relations Act (LRA) protects employees from unfair dismissal and unfair labour practices. If you have been dismissed, demoted, or disciplined unfairly, you can refer a dispute to the CCMA (Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration) — free of charge.
The Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA) sets minimum standards: working hours (max 45 hrs/week), overtime pay, leave entitlements (21 days annual, 30 days sick per 3 years), and maternity leave (4 months, unpaid but UIF-eligible).
The Employment Equity Act prohibits unfair discrimination based on race, gender, disability, and other grounds. If you have been passed over for promotion, paid less, or treated differently based on any protected characteristic — this is actionable.
Constructive dismissal is when an employer makes your working conditions so intolerable that you are effectively forced to resign. This is treated as an unfair dismissal under SA law. Document everything before you leave — it builds your case.
Bullying and harassment at work can be referred to the CCMA as an unfair labour practice. Sexual harassment is covered under the Code of Good Practice on the Handling of Sexual Harassment Cases.
CCMA Contact: 0861 16 2762 or ccma.org.za — Free dispute resolution. No lawyers needed. All workers regardless of salary are eligible.
Step 4
Boundaries at Work — Protect Your Energy
Boundaries at work are not about doing less. They are about doing what matters — sustainably. Without them, the workplace will take everything you have and ask for more.
🚧 Why Work Boundaries Are Hard
The Black tax hustle trap. Many Black South African professionals grew up watching their parents overwork to survive. The message was clear: you have to work twice as hard to get half as far. While this drive creates success, it also creates a deep inability to stop — even when your body and mind are begging you to.
Fear of being seen as "difficult." For women and for Black professionals in particular, saying no, pushing back, or asserting limits is frequently interpreted as aggression or a bad attitude. The fear of this perception keeps millions of people overextended.
Identity tied to productivity. If your worth as a person is connected to how much you produce, then resting feels like failing. This belief system — often formed in childhood — is one of the primary drivers of burnout.
The "strong one" tax. If you are known as capable, you will attract more work. High performers are often punished with more responsibility without proportional compensation — because the organisation knows you'll do it.

Career Transition & Finding Purpose at Work

🔮 The Ikigai Framework — Find Your Intersection

Ikigai is a Japanese concept meaning "reason for being." It sits at the intersection of four questions. Where all four overlap is where sustainable, meaningful work lives.

What do you love?
What are you good at?
What does the world need?
What can you be paid for?
