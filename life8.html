Step 2
Body Image — Your Relationship With Your Own Body
Body image is not about how you look. It is about how you feel about how you look — and those are very different things. Both can be worked on.
🧠 Understanding Body Image

Body image is the mental picture you have of your body combined with your feelings, thoughts, and beliefs about it. Negative body image is not vanity — it is a real psychological experience that affects mental health, relationships, eating, sexual confidence, and self-worth.

Media and colonialism. The standard of "beautiful" in South Africa is still heavily influenced by Eurocentric ideals that were imposed through colonialism. Dark skin, natural hair, full bodies, and African features were systematically devalued. Healing body image for many South Africans means actively unlearning a colonised standard.
Social media. The average person sees hundreds of curated, filtered, and often surgically altered images per day. This creates a comparison standard that no unfiltered human body can meet — including the person in the photo.
The difference between weight and health. BMI was created by a mathematician in 1832 to describe populations — not individuals. It does not measure health. Healthy people come in every size. Size is not a moral category.
Body neutrality vs body positivity. Body positivity asks you to love your body every day. Body neutrality says: your body doesn't have to be loved — it just has to be respected. You don't owe your body admiration. You owe it care.

Step 3
Sexual Health — What You Need to Know
Sexual health is not just about avoiding disease. It is about pleasure, safety, consent, and the right to make informed decisions about your own body.
⚖️ Your Sexual Rights — South African Law
The right to say no. You have the right to refuse sexual activity at any time — before, during, or after intimacy has begun. Consent can be withdrawn at any point. This includes within a marriage.
Marital rape is a crime. Section 3 of the Sexual Offences Act 32 of 2007 makes marital rape illegal in South Africa. No marriage or relationship gives another person ownership of your body.
Age of consent is 16. Sexual activity with anyone under 16 is statutory rape regardless of perceived consent. Both parties aged 12–16 engaging with each other is handled under a separate provision.
Access to contraception. You have the right to access contraception at any public clinic without your partner's permission, without your parents' knowledge if you are 12+, and without judgment.
Access to abortion. The Choice on Termination of Pregnancy Act allows abortion on request up to 12 weeks. Between 13–20 weeks under specified conditions. This is a legal right. Public health facilities are required to provide this service.
Confidentiality. Your sexual and reproductive health information is confidential. Healthcare providers are bound by confidentiality except where there is risk to life.

Step 4
STIs & HIV — Facts Without Shame
South Africa has the largest HIV epidemic in the world. Knowing the facts removes the fear. Testing removes the uncertainty. Treatment changes everything.
❤️ HIV — What You Must Know
HIV is a manageable chronic condition. With antiretroviral treatment (ART), people living with HIV can live full, long lives and cannot transmit the virus to partners (U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable).
PrEP. Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis is a daily medication taken by HIV-negative people that is 99% effective at preventing HIV transmission. Available free at public clinics in South Africa.
PEP. Post-Exposure Prophylaxis must be started within 72 hours of potential exposure. It is a 28-day course of medication. Available at public hospital emergency departments.
Test regularly. Every sexually active person should test for HIV at least once a year — more often with multiple partners. Testing takes 15 minutes and is free at all public clinics.
HIV status is private. You are under no legal obligation to disclose your status to family, employers, or anyone except sexual partners (who you must notify). Discrimination based on HIV status is illegal in South Africa.

🛡️ Protection — How to Reduce Risk
🏥 Get Tested Regularly

HIV + STI testing at any public clinic — free. Pap smear every 3 years from age 21 (or 3 years after first sexual activity). Breast self-exam monthly. Know your status.

🎀 Use Protection Consistently

Male and female condoms are the only contraception that also protects against STIs. PrEP adds an additional layer of HIV protection. Dual protection = condom + another contraceptive method.

💬 Communicate With Partners

Conversations about testing status, contraception preferences, and consent are a sign of a mature, respectful partnership. If a partner refuses this conversation — that tells you something important.

Step 5
Reproductive Wellness
Your reproductive health is your health. Cycle awareness, fertility, menopause, prostate health — know your body across every season of life.
🌸 Menstrual Health
A normal cycle is 21–35 days. Period duration: 3–7 days. Blood loss: 30–80ml per cycle. Anything significantly outside this range consistently is worth discussing with a healthcare provider.
Period pain (dysmenorrhoea) is common but debilitating pain is not normal and should be investigated. Conditions like endometriosis — which affects 1 in 10 women — are frequently dismissed and under-diagnosed, particularly in Black women.
PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects up to 10% of women. Symptoms include irregular periods, excess hair growth, acne, and difficulty conceiving. It is manageable with the right care and often goes undiagnosed for years.
Menopause typically begins between 45–55. Perimenopause (the transition) can last years. Symptoms include hot flashes, mood changes, irregular periods, and sleep disruption. Hormone therapy and lifestyle adjustments can significantly improve quality of life.
🔵 Men's Sexual & Reproductive Health
Testicular self-exam. Monthly self-examination is the best early detection for testicular cancer, which is most common in men aged 15–35. Check for lumps, changes in size or shape, or heaviness.
Prostate health. Prostate cancer is the most common cancer in South African men. Black men are at higher risk. PSA screening from age 40 (or 35 with family history) is recommended. Early detection is highly treatable.
Erectile dysfunction (ED). ED affects up to 40% of men over 40 — but increasingly younger men. It is often a symptom of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, anxiety, or relationship factors. It is treatable. Seeking help is not weakness.
Male fertility. 40-50% of fertility issues are attributable to male factors. Sperm count, motility, and morphology can be affected by heat, lifestyle, and stress. Fertility is a shared responsibility.
Circumcision and HIV. Medical male circumcision reduces HIV transmission risk by approximately 60% in heterosexual men. It is not a substitute for condoms or PrEP — but an additional layer of protection.
💛 Intimacy & Sexual Wellbeing

Sexual wellbeing is a recognized component of overall health by the World Health Organization. It includes the ability to have pleasurable, safe sexual experiences — free from coercion, discrimination, and violence.

Low libido is one of the most common — and least discussed — sexual health issues. It can be caused by stress, medication (especially antidepressants, contraceptives), hormonal changes, relationship issues, trauma, or depression. It is not a personal failure.
Pain during sex (dyspareunia or vaginismus) affects millions of women and is consistently under-reported and under-treated. It is a medical condition — not a psychological problem to push through. A gynaecologist or pelvic floor physiotherapist can help.
Sexual trauma recovery. Sexual trauma fundamentally changes one's relationship with their body and with intimacy. Healing is possible — but it requires safety, time, and often professional support. There is no timeline for this.
